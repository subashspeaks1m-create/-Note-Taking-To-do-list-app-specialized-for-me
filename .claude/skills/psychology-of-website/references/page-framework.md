# The KISS Page Framework, Section by Section

Default order: Hero → Problem + solution (×2) → Video testimonials → Price justification + why us → Image/text testimonials → Final CTA. Each section exists to answer a question in the visitor's head. If you can't say which question a section answers, cut it or rewrite it.

## 1. Hero

**Answers:** "Is this for me? What is it?"

- **Headline:** answer at least one of: *What do you do? How does it work? What do you sell? How does the customer benefit?* The strongest heroes speak to the ICP's desire directly ("Get your old self back. Naturally.") or name the enemy ("Stop drinking sugar. Try the real thing.").
- **Subline:** a plain explanation of what it is and how it helps, in one or two lines.
- **Proof snippet:** star rating plus review count, customer count, or "Trusted by 400 local homeowners". The visitor gets a reason to believe before they've scrolled at all.
- **Primary CTA:** specific wording, with high contrast.
- **Visual:** the product in use or the outcome being enjoyed. Avoid abstract mood imagery, which doesn't help a stranger understand.
- **Match the traffic source.** If people arrive from an ad about hot flashes, the hero should say "hot flashes" or something close to it. A mismatch between the ad and the hero makes people bounce.

**Mistakes:** clever-but-vague headlines, company-centric lines ("Welcome to Acme Solutions"), three competing CTAs, a slider rotating through five messages.

## 2. Problem + how the product helps (usually two consecutive sections)

**Answers:** "Does it solve *my* problem?"

- Name the pain in the ICP's own words. Make them nod: "Waking up at 3am and staring at the ceiling?"
- Show the product as the fix: *"Going through X? Choose Y and get Z."*
- Back up the mechanism briefly: why it works (ingredients, process, method), in plain language.
- The second consecutive section usually goes deeper on one specific pain or use case, reinforcing "we are for you." Example: "Hit snooze on sleepless nights. Maximize rest for optimal recovery."
- Quick proof bars fit well here ("Clinically validated doses · Clean ingredients · Formulated by nutritional scientists").

## 3. Testimonial section (video-first)

**Answers:** "Does it actually work for people like me?"

- Video testimonials from real customers who look and sound like the ICP, telling a before-and-after story in under about 45 seconds each.
- Caption each with the name, a detail that makes them relatable ("mom of 3, Ohio"), and the headline result ("Hot flashes gone in 4 weeks").
- If there's no video, use photo reviews with a pull-quote headline.
- Every testimonial must be real. See `social-proof.md` for how to get real ones fast.

## 4. Price justification + why we're better

**Answers:** "Why so expensive? Why not the cheap alternative?"

Address the price before the visitor objects to it. Options, usually two or three combined:
- **Quality standards:** "World's highest quality standards", certifications, testing, sourcing.
- **Us vs them comparison table:** cold-pressed vs from concentrate, real fruit vs added sugar, licensed vs unlicensed, fixed price vs hourly surprises.
- **Process transparency:** how it's made or how the service is delivered.
- **Cost reframing:** cost per day, or the cost of the problem continuing, or what it replaces.
- **Scale proof:** "13.4M bottles sold · 3.3M customers · 104k reviews." Popularity justifies price: so many people paid it, so they must know something.
- **Authority:** doctors, nutritionists, engineers, or industry experts who recommend it.

## 5. Second testimonial section (image/text)

**Answers:** "Am I sure? Are there people like me who were happy?"

- Use a different format and different people from section 3, such as a grid of text or photo reviews, a press quote strip, or expert endorsements.
- Include specific results and a variety of ICP sub-types.
- Don't overdo it. Two or three proof blocks across the page is the sweet spot. Six identical walls of praise start to look fabricated.

## 6. Final CTA

**Answers:** "Okay, what do I do now? Is it safe to try?"

- Restate the outcome as a question or invitation: "Ready for better sleep? Try Knock."
- Use risk reversal: money-back guarantee, free shipping, free quote, cancel anytime.
- Keep it to one button with specific wording. Repeat the offer (price, bundle) if it applies.
- Brand line to close the story: "Choose real. Choose Raw."

## 7. Optional sections (add only when they answer a real question)

| Section | Answers | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Trust bar ("as featured in", key numbers) | "Is this legit?" | Right under the hero |
| Product range / bestsellers | "Which one do I pick?" | After problem/solution or after proof |
| How it works in 3 steps | "Is it complicated?" | After problem/solution (great for services and SaaS) |
| FAQ / objection handling | "But what about...?" | Before the final CTA |
| Guarantee block | "What if it doesn't work?" | Next to the price or the final CTA |
| Founder story | "Who's behind this? Can I trust them?" | Mid-page, especially for new brands with little proof |
| Repeated mini-CTAs | Catches people who are ready early | After each major proof block |

## 8. Variants by business type

**DTC / physical product:** the framework as written. Lean heavily on reviews, scale numbers, ingredients and quality, and us vs them. The CTA goes to the product page or checkout.

**Local service (plumber, dentist, roofer, lawyer):** the hero names the service, the area, and the speed ("Burst pipe? Licensed plumbers in Austin, at your door in 60 minutes."). Proof is Google reviews, years in business, licenses and insurance, and before/after photos. Price justification is upfront pricing, guarantees, and "no surprise fees". The CTA is call now or book online, with a click-to-call button on mobile.

**SaaS / app:** the hero states the outcome plus the product category ("Cut churn 30% with automated win-back emails"). Use product screenshots or a short demo in the problem/solution section. Proof is customer logos, case-study metrics, and G2 ratings. Price justification is ROI or time saved, and a comparison to the manual way or to competitors. The CTA is a free trial or demo.

**Agency / B2B service:** the hero states the specific result for the specific client type ("We fill dental clinics' calendars with Google Ads"). Proof is case studies with numbers and recognizable client logos. "How we work" in three steps lowers perceived risk. The CTA is book a call, ideally with a qualifying hook ("free ad account audit").

**Coach / info product:** the hero states the transformation. Proof is student results and the founder's credibility. Price justification is what it's worth compared to the cost of staying stuck. A guarantee is critical.

## 9. Common structural mistakes

- A features grid before the visitor knows who it's for.
- Proof buried at the very bottom, or missing entirely.
- The price shown with no justification above it.
- A navigation bar with ten links pulling visitors away from the funnel (simplify it on landing pages).
- The final section is just a footer, with no closing CTA.
- Sending the main CTA to a quiz, a contact page with ten fields, or another site, when a direct path would work.
- Copying a mega-brand's minimal site (Dior, Apple, Shopify) for a business with no brand recognition.
- Visual effects (scroll video, 3D) that delay or hide the hero message, especially on mobile.
