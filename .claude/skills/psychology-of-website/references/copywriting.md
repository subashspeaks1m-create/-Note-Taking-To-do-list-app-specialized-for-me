# Copywriting for High-Converting Websites

Copy is what sells. Design makes it easy to read. Write like a sniper, not a machine gunner: one target, one precise shot per section. A machine gunner sprays the crowd and hits little. A sniper aims at one person and lands.

## 1. Rules

1. **Use the buyer's language, not the brand's.** Lift phrases straight from reviews and forums (see `icp-research.md`).
2. **Specific beats general.** "4 weeks", "81,756 reviews", "at your door in 60 minutes", "formulated by nutritional scientists".
3. **Outcome first, mechanism second, features last.** A feature only matters as a reason to believe the outcome.
4. **Write to one person.** Use "you", never "customers" or "users".
5. **Short headlines, explanatory sublines.** The headline must land in one glance on a phone. The subline does the explaining.
6. **Each section's copy answers its question.** Write the question above the section in your draft. If the copy doesn't answer it, rewrite it.
7. **Stay on the single narrative angle.** Each headline should echo the page's story (e.g., every Raw headline plays on sugar vs real fruit).
8. **Claims must be true and supportable.** Numbers, health claims, "best", "#1", and certifications need a real source. If you don't have the number, ask the user or mark it `[NEEDS DATA]`. Health, finance, and legal niches carry extra regulatory risk, so be conservative with claims there.
9. **Read it aloud as the ICP.** If any line makes the inner voice say "so what?" or "says who?", fix it.

## 2. Headline patterns

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Restore the old self | "Get your old self back. Naturally." |
| Stop X, start Y (name the enemy) | "Stop drinking sugar. Try the real thing." |
| Name the pain, offer the fix | "Hit snooze on sleepless nights." |
| Outcome + timeframe | "Hot flashes gone in 4 weeks." |
| Outcome for a specific who | "We fill dental clinics' calendars with Google Ads." |
| Question that makes them nod | "Tired of juices that taste like fruit but are mostly sugar?" |
| Us vs them in one line | "Sugar should come from the fruit, not the packet." |
| Service + place + speed | "Licensed plumbers in Austin. At your door in 60 minutes." |

Generate 5 to 10 options per key headline, then pick by asking: *would the ICP stop scrolling for this?*

## 3. Section copy patterns

- **Problem/solution:** "Going through **[pain in their words]**? **[Product]** **[mechanism in plain words]**, so you can **[outcome]**."
- **Proof intro:** "**[Number]** people like you chose **[product]**. Here's what they say."
- **Price justification:** "Why **[price]**? Because **[quality standard]**, **[sourcing or process]**, and **[what it replaces]**." Or reframe it: "That's **[price ÷ days]** a day for **[outcome]**."
- **Us vs them:** a two-column table with **Us** and **Them**, 4 to 6 rows, each row a concrete difference the ICP cares about.
- **Guarantee:** "Try it for **[N]** days. If **[outcome]** doesn't happen, **[refund or redo]**. No questions."
- **Final CTA:** "Ready for **[outcome]**? **[CTA verb] [product]**."

## 4. CTA copy

- Say what happens: "Book my free quote", "Get my first bottle", "Start my 14-day trial". Never just "Submit" or "Click here".
- First person ("my") often beats second person ("your") on buttons. It's worth testing.
- Keep one primary CTA across the page. Secondary CTAs (like "See ingredients") should look visually lighter.
- Add a friction reducer under the button: "Free shipping · 60-day guarantee", "No credit card needed", "Takes 30 seconds".

## 5. Words to cut

These sound like generic AI copy and make a page feel like every other page: *unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, seamless, revolutionize, game-changer, cutting-edge, next level, in today's fast-paced world, look no further, one-stop shop, solutions, synergy, world-class* (unless it's backed by proof), *best-in-class*, and *innovative*.

Also cut throat-clearing openers ("Welcome to our website", "We are a company that..."), and adjectives that don't add a fact.

## 6. Worked examples

These come from teardowns of real sites. The figures are as they appeared on those sites at the time of analysis, so treat them as illustrations, not current facts.

### Happy Mammoth (hormone balance supplement; reported $100M+ in sales)

The monologue walkthrough, as the ICP (a woman with hormonal imbalance who clicked an ad):

1. **Hero:** "Get your old self back. Naturally." → *"Before this I felt really good. This speaks to me. It's for me."* Then 81,756 five-star reviews → *"So many people bought it. It must work."*
2. **Trust bar:** "Ingredients in scientifically validated doses · Clean ingredients · Formulated by nutritional scientists · 3.3M+ customers in 36 countries." → *"Formulated by scientists. Wow."*
3. **Press:** "Featured in..." → *"They've been in all these publications."*
4. **Bestsellers:** the prices appear → *"Hmm, a bit expensive..."* Then immediately: "13.4M bottles sold · 3.3M customers · 104k reviews." → *"...but this many people paid it."*
5. **Testimonials:** "4 weeks and my hot flashes are gone." → *"Kathy, Jenny, and Andrea all vouch for a $90 bottle."*
6. **Five reasons to try us + world's highest quality standards** → *"Other brands cut corners to make it cheap. This one doesn't."*
7. **More proof, then doctors and nutritionists recommend it** → *"Doctors suggest it."* By the end, she's sold.

The one weakness: the final step is "Take a quiz" instead of a direct buy CTA. A sold visitor should get the shortest path to checkout.

**Lesson:** the hero just says "this is for you", and every section after it is another affirmation that removes one more doubt, especially the doubt about price.

### Try Knock (sleep gummies; simple site, strong sales)

The structure: hero (what it is) → "Hit snooze on sleepless nights. Maximize rest for optimal recovery." (problem + how it helps, reinforcing "we're for you") → video testimonials → more content → image-based testimonials → "Ready for better sleep? Try Knock." It's the KISS framework almost exactly, and it shows a simple site can sell.

### Raw (cold-pressed juice; redesign example)

**Narrative angle:** sugar. Most packaged juice is sugar water. Raw is real fruit.

| Section | Copy |
|---|---|
| Hero | "Stop drinking sugar. Try the real thing." |
| Problem/solution | "Taste the freshness, not just the sugar." (with flavors like Alphonso mango and sugarcane) |
| Us vs them belief | "Sugar should come from the fruit, not from the packet." |
| Proof bar | "Real fruit · Cold pressed · No artificial colors" |
| Video testimonials | "Real people. Real stories. For the people who choose natural over chemicals." |
| Product range | "Shop the current range" |
| Price justification | "Make the healthy choice. Don't cheap out on your health." |
| Us vs them table | "What you get: Raw vs them" (cold-pressed vs concentrate, and so on) |
| Image testimonials | "Real people, real Raw." |
| Final CTA | "Choose real. Choose Raw." → "Buy Raw" |

**Lesson:** one angle, carried through every headline, turns a forgettable product grid into a story with an enemy and a hero.
