# Social Proof: Getting It, Placing It, Keeping It Real

Without testimonials and reviews, almost nobody buys from a brand they don't know. Proof is the part of the page that lets a stranger borrow other people's confidence.

## The non-negotiable: proof must be real

Don't create fake customers, reviews, star ratings, or testimonial videos, and don't use AI-generated people presented as real buyers. Three reasons:

1. **It's illegal in major markets.** The US FTC's Trade Regulation Rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (in force since October 2024) bans fake reviews and testimonials, explicitly including AI-generated ones, with civil penalties per violation. The UK's Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 bans fake reviews as well, and many other countries have similar consumer-protection rules.
2. **It destroys the client's brand when it's discovered**, and reverse image search, review-platform audits, and customers comparing notes mean it usually is.
3. **It fails the client.** Fake proof can lift a page for a short while, but it builds the business on something that can collapse.

If a user asks for fabricated testimonials, explain briefly why you won't, then go straight to the fast, legitimate options below. Most clients have more real proof than they think.

**Allowed:**
- **Clearly labeled placeholders** in mockups and pitch drafts, e.g. `[PLACEHOLDER: real customer review goes here]`, listed in the handoff as "replace before launch".
- **AI-generated imagery** for product shots, lifestyle scenes, backgrounds, and illustrations that don't pretend to be a specific real customer.
- **Real reviews redesigned** into attractive cards, pull quotes, or photo collages, with the customer's permission.

## Fast ways to get real proof

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Has past customers, no collected reviews | Ask them. Offer a small thank-you (discount, free add-on, ebook, gift) for an **honest** review or a short selfie video, disclosed as incentivized, and never conditioned on it being positive. Send a 3-question prompt: what was life like before? What changed? Who would you recommend it to? |
| Has reviews scattered elsewhere | Pull them in, with permission, from Google, Amazon, Etsy, Facebook, DMs, and emails. Screenshots of real DMs, with names blurred if necessary, feel very authentic. |
| Brand new, no customers | Run a small beta: give the product or service to 5–20 people in the ICP in exchange for honest feedback and permission to quote them. |
| B2B / services | Write 1 to 3 short case studies: client, problem, what you did, the result with a number. Even one strong case study beats ten vague quotes. |
| No customers *and* no time | Lean on the other proof types below and be honest about being new ("Launching with our first 100 customers. Here's our guarantee.") |

## Other proof types (use them in rotation)

- **Numbers:** customers served, units sold, years in business, jobs completed, average rating.
- **Authority:** credentials, licenses, certifications, expert or doctor endorsements (real and permitted), awards.
- **Press and logos:** "As featured in" (only real features) and client logos (with permission).
- **Process transparency:** how it's made, sourced, or tested; behind-the-scenes photos or video.
- **Founder credibility:** who's behind it and why they're qualified, told as a short story.
- **Risk reversal:** money-back guarantee, free trial, free quote, "pay after the job is done".
- **Live signals:** "1,200 orders this month", recent-purchase notices, but only when the data is real.

## Placement and rhythm

- A proof snippet in the hero (rating plus count).
- A trust bar right under the hero (numbers, press, credentials).
- Video testimonials after the problem/solution section.
- Scale numbers or authority right after the price appears.
- Image or text testimonials before the final CTA.
- A guarantee next to the final CTA.

Vary the format and the people each time. Feature testimonials from people who match the ICP, and lead each with its specific result ("Hot flashes gone in 4 weeks") rather than generic praise ("Great product!").
