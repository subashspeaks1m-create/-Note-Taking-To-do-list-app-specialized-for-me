# ICP Research: Get Into the Buyer's Shoes

Increasing conversion rate is mostly understanding the niche and the product better than anyone else, then writing the page from that understanding. This file shows how to build that understanding quickly.

## 1. The immersion exercise

Answer these questions as vividly as you can, in the first person where it helps. Generic answers ("they want quality") are worthless. Push for sensory, specific detail. This used to take hours or days of interviews. You can do a strong first pass in minutes, then sharpen it with real customer language (section 2).

Use this prompt on yourself, or hand it to the user to run anywhere. Replace X with the product or service, and Y with the person:

> I'm planning to sell **X** to **Y**. Put yourself completely in this person's shoes.
> - What does their day-to-day life look like? Make the imagery vivid and colorful so I can feel it.
> - What problem pushes them to look for something like X? When does it hurt most, at what moment of the day or week?
> - What have they already tried, and why did it disappoint them?
> - What do they secretly want, the outcome behind the outcome?
> - What are they afraid of: wasting money, looking foolish, side effects, being scammed, the hassle of switching?
> - What would they need to see or hear to trust a new brand?
> - Why would they choose to buy X? Why would they *not*?
> - What exact words and phrases would they use to describe their problem to a friend?

Examples of who Y can be: a marathon runner choosing race-specific shoes, a woman with hormonal imbalance, a homeowner with a burst pipe at 11pm, a zookeeper hunting for a marketing agency, a SaaS founder whose churn is climbing.

**Why vividness matters:** you can't write "Get your old self back" unless you've felt what it's like to miss your old self. Specific emotional detail is what turns into headlines that land.

## 2. Voice-of-customer mining

Sharpen the immersion with real language whenever you can:

- **Where to look:** product reviews (Amazon, Trustpilot, G2, Google Maps, App Store) for the product *and its competitors*, especially 3-star and 4-star reviews, where people explain what's missing; Reddit and niche forums; YouTube comments; Facebook groups; Q&A sections; the client's own support emails and sales call notes.
- **What to collect:** recurring phrases for (a) pains, (b) desired outcomes, (c) objections and hesitations, (d) what tipped them into buying, (e) comparisons to alternatives.
- **How much:** a handful of good sources is enough. You're looking for repetition. When the same phrase shows up three times, it's headline material.
- **If there's no web access:** ask the user to paste a few real reviews, or list the objections they hear most often on sales calls.

Keep the buyer's exact wording. "I'm so tired of waking up at 3am" beats any polished rewrite.

## 3. Turning research into the internal question map

Convert the research into an ordered list of the questions this ICP will have while scrolling, then assign each to a section:

| Order | ICP's question (in their words) | Section that answers it | Evidence we have |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Is this for women like me going through menopause?" | Hero | Headline + 81k reviews |
| 2 | "Will it actually fix my hot flashes?" | Problem/solution | Ingredient explanation, 4-week claim |
| 3 | "Why $90 a bottle?" | Price justification | Clinical doses, quality standards |
| ... | ... | ... | ... |

Any question without a section is a leak where visitors drop off. Any section without a question is filler. The map is also how you'll audit the finished page.

## 4. When the client doesn't know their ICP

New businesses often say "our product is for everyone". Help them narrow it down:

1. Ask who has bought (or almost bought) so far. Look for patterns in age, job, situation, and trigger event.
2. Ask who gets the *biggest, fastest* result from the product. That's usually the best ICP.
3. Ask who already spends money trying to solve this problem. Buyers with a budget and urgency beat browsers.
4. Draft 2 or 3 candidate ICPs with a one-paragraph portrait each, then recommend one, explaining why (urgency, ability to pay, ease of reaching them, how well the product fits).
5. Reassure the client that niching down doesn't shrink sales. The page becomes magnetic to the core buyer, and adjacent buyers (people shopping for a mother, a sister, a partner) still recognize themselves.

## 5. ICP brief format

```markdown
# ICP Brief: [Business / Product]

**Who they are:** [one vivid sentence: age range, situation, trigger]
**A day in their life:** [3–5 sentences, sensory and specific]
**The pain (their words):** "[quote]", "[quote]", "[quote]"
**What they've tried and why it failed:** [...]
**What they really want:** [the outcome behind the outcome]
**Fears and objections:** [ranked, most deal-breaking first]
**What earns their trust:** [proof types that would convince them]
**Where they come from:** [ad, search, referral, social; affects the hero]
**Narrative angle options:** 1) ... 2) ... 3) ... → Recommended: ... because ...
**Internal question map:** [table from section 3]
```
